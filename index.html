How I Lead

Four principles I run BI platforms by.

01

I run platforms, not dashboards.

A dashboard is an artifact; a platform is what makes the next thousand dashboards trustworthy. At Jackson, I owned the Tableau platform end-to-end as site administrator — dev / QA / production structure, permissions, versioning, and data stewardship across two enterprise sites. The work executives don't see is the work that makes the dashboards executives do see worth opening.

02

I build the operating rhythm.

Tools alone don't change adoption — cadence does. I co-lead a BI Community of Practice with bi-weekly office hours and monthly enablement sessions, partner with the internal training team on Power BI and Fabric curriculum, and run the QA and intake practices that keep production reporting reliable. Governance only works when the governed path is the easiest path.

03

I translate, both ways.

The bi-weekly Power BI demos I run with VPs and C-suite stakeholders at Jackson are half-engineering, half-translation. Senior leaders need the number, the trend, and the caveat in plain language; the engineering team needs the requirement disambiguated before they build. Sitting in the middle of that handoff is where most reporting programs fail and where I spend most of my time.

04

I grow the people around me.

I coach 7–8 BI analysts and engineers at Jackson on Power BI, Fabric, and reporting craft. Outside the day job, I represent Jackson as a Talent Ambassador at Michigan State University, co-chair the ASCENT BRG — membership grew 100%, first all-BRG summit in Lansing with 100+ attendees — and serve as VP of the Kelley Direct MBA Marketing & Growth Association.
