Context

Context

At Infosys, BI enablement operated at a scale where training could not be treated as a one-time presentation. New trainees and professionals arrived in global learning cohorts of 50 to 300+ people with different levels of data, SQL, visualization, and business-reporting maturity. The goal was not just to explain tools like Power BI, Tableau, Cognos, SSIS, SSRS, Informatica, Snowflake, Qlik, Pentaho, and SQL. The goal was to move people from exposure to practical readiness.

That meant every cohort needed a learning rhythm and an operating backbone: classroom setup, infrastructure readiness, attendance and leave tracking, instruction, hands-on exams, capstone projects, evaluation, doubt resolution, feedback, and a way to convert repeated questions into better curriculum for the next group.

The Problem

The Problem

The work was not hard because of one difficult topic. It was hard because of throughput, variation, and continuity:

  • Each cohort arrived with uneven readiness — some learners needed core BI concepts, while others needed advanced reporting, data modeling, or production-support context.
  • Classroom ownership created an operational layer beyond teaching: attendance, leave requests, learner readiness, exam setup, and infrastructure preparation had to be handled before learning could run smoothly.
  • Hands-on exams and assignments created a second operating load: submissions had to be reviewed, patterns had to be diagnosed, and feedback had to be specific enough to improve the next attempt.
  • Capstone projects needed leadership, structure, evaluation criteria, and mentoring so learners could connect classroom skills to end-to-end BI delivery.
  • Doubts repeated across cohorts, but if they were handled only verbally, the same questions returned in the next learning group.
  • Tool coverage was broad, so the training model had to separate transferable BI thinking from tool-specific mechanics.
  • Learning content needed to outlast a single instructor or cohort and become reusable institutional material.

The leadership challenge was turning repeated training delivery into an operating system for learning quality.

My Role

My Role

I owned classroom and cohort operations in addition to instruction. That included preparing the infrastructure for hands-on exams, setting up and running evaluations, tracking attendance and leaves, reviewing learner work, leading capstone projects, resolving questions during and after sessions, authoring curriculum, and mentoring learners beyond formal classroom time. In parallel, I served as a Production BI Support SME across programs, which kept the training grounded in real reporting problems rather than classroom-only examples.

The role required more than teaching tool features. It required planning the cohort flow, maintaining quality across 50 to 300+ professionals at a time, handling classroom operations, identifying recurring gaps, and converting those gaps into better modules, examples, exercises, capstone guidance, and feedback loops.

Approach

Approach

Four operating decisions shaped the work: prepare the environment, teach the concept, force practice, evaluate the evidence, and preserve the learning for the next cohort.

  • 01

    Run cohorts as a managed learning operation, not a lecture series.

    Each cohort needed a path from onboarding to readiness: classroom setup, attendance and leave tracking, infrastructure preparation, topic framing, live instruction, guided practice, hands-on exams, capstone work, review, and feedback. Treating the cohort as an operation made it possible to manage progress across many learners without reducing training to passive attendance.

  • 02

    Use exams, assignments, and capstones as the real diagnostic.

    Submitted work showed what learners actually understood. Reviewing hands-on exams, assignments, and capstone outputs made hidden gaps visible: weak data-modeling assumptions, unclear KPI definitions, poor visual hierarchy, or tool mechanics memorized without reporting judgment. That evidence shaped the next round of explanation and examples.

  • 03

    Turn repeated questions into reusable curriculum.

    When the same doubts appeared across cohorts, the answer could not stay in one session. Those patterns became clearer examples, stronger assignments, improved exam preparation, better capstone guidance, improved modules, and better sequencing. This is how delivery scaled: every cohort improved the system for the next one.

  • 04

    Keep the training connected to production BI reality.

    Because I also worked as a Production BI Support SME, the training could include what breaks in real projects: refresh failures, unclear requirements, report performance, governance, stakeholder interpretation, and data-quality caveats. That connection made the learning more useful than tool instruction alone.

What I Built

What I Built

  • 800+ live and virtual BI/data sessions delivered to 10,000+ professionals globally across reporting, visualization, integration, and data-platform tools.
  • Global learning cohorts of 50 to 300+ professionals managed through classroom ownership, attendance and leave tracking, infrastructure preparation, instruction, hands-on exams, evaluation, and mentoring.
  • Capstone project leadership: creating project structures, guiding learners through applied BI/data work, reviewing outputs, and helping connect classroom skills to end-to-end delivery.
  • Repeated cohort operating practices: structured delivery, hands-on work cycles, question handling, assignment review, evaluation, and feedback patterns that supported learners at different readiness levels.
  • 4 core BI learning modules for Infosys Lex, with 2 extended to Springboard, turning repeated training needs into reusable curriculum assets.
  • Additional course content across 10+ BI and data tools, including Power BI, Tableau, IBM Cognos, SSIS, SSRS, Informatica, Snowflake, Qlik, Pentaho, SQL, and MongoDB.
  • Mentoring support for learners and 7+ post-hire colleagues in BI tools and methods, extending the enablement model beyond formal classroom cohorts.
Outcome

Outcome

The work scaled BI capability through a repeatable training system rather than one-off sessions. Across Infosys, I delivered 800+ live and virtual sessions to 10,000+ professionals globally, authored 4 core BI modules for Infosys Lex, supported 2 modules extended to Springboard, and received Infosys INSTA Awards in 2018 and 2020 for training innovation and analytics enablement.

  • Learners received structured instruction plus hands-on exam setup, work review, evaluation, capstone guidance, and mentoring, not just tool demonstrations.
  • Classroom operations — attendance, leave tracking, infrastructure readiness, and evaluation logistics — were handled as part of the enablement system.
  • Repeated questions, exam patterns, assignment patterns, and capstone issues became reusable examples, modules, and content improvements.
  • Training remained connected to real production BI issues through parallel Production BI Support SME work.
  • Mentoring extended beyond formal cohorts to support learners and 7+ post-hire colleagues developing BI capability.

Reflection

Reflection

The evaluation loop is where leadership becomes visible.

Large-scale enablement is not only about presenting clearly. It is about seeing what learners produce in exams, assignments, and capstones; spotting patterns across many submissions; and helping people correct their reasoning. That work taught me to lead through evidence, not assumption.

Reusable curriculum is the only way training scales responsibly.

When the same question appears across cohorts, the answer should become part of the system. The best training programs get stronger with repetition because every cohort reveals what the curriculum still needs to explain.

This is where my enablement leadership started: not with a formal management title, but with repeated responsibility for groups of learners, the quality of their work, and the system that helped the next group move faster.
